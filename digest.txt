① Andrej Karpathy · AI Researcher & Educator

Karpathy 推荐了"Farzapedia"作为基于 LLM 的个人记忆的优秀案例，认为这种显式、可导航的记忆方式优于主流 AI 的模糊个性化路径。他还提出，AI 正在赋能公民监督政府，使政府行为更加透明可问责，这与历史上由政府主导社会透明化的逻辑恰好相反。此外，他推广了"idea file"的概念——在 LLM agent 时代，分享想法本身就已足够，无需附上具体代码。

---

Karpathy highlighted "Farzapedia" as a strong example of explicit, navigable personal memory built on LLMs, contrasting it with opaque AI personalization approaches. He also shared thoughts on AI enabling citizens to hold governments accountable by making governance more legible — a reversal of the historical norm. Additionally, he reflected on a viral tweet, promoting the concept of an "idea file" where sharing ideas (rather than code) is sufficient in the LLM agent era.

② Aaron Levie · CEO @ Box

Levie 指出，即便是最先进的 AI 模型在实际应用中也难以满足需求，因为每位用户都有独特的工作流程和领域知识，模型无法预先掌握这一切。他还表示，随着模型在工具调用上的能力提升以及 context window 的扩大和稳定，agent 的设计可以更接近人类的实际工作方式，不再需要为弥补模型缺陷而采用各种奇怪的变通方案。

---

Levie observed that even the most advanced AI models fall short in practice because every user has unique workflows and domain knowledge that models can't pre-load. He also noted that as models improve at tool use and context windows grow more reliable, agent architecture can now mirror how humans actually work, eliminating the awkward hacks previously needed to compensate for model limitations.

🔗 https://x.com/levie/status/2040650799550722243

🎙 Podcast

AI for Atoms: How Periodic Labs is Revolutionizing Materials Engineering with Co-Founder Liam Fedus

ChatGPT联合创造者、前OpenAI后训练副总裁Liam Fedus创立了Periodic Labs，专注于为材料科学和化学构建AI基础模型，将前沿AI技术应用于原子层面的物理世界。值得关注的是，Fedus认为AI革命若不与实体实验直接结合，就无法真正加速科学进步，单靠语言模型远远不够。他拥有物理学背景，曾在Google Brain参与混合专家架构和Transformer时代的早期工作，并指出只有近年来推理能力、测试时推断及智能体工具使用的突破，才使这一物理世界AI愿景在技术上成为可能。他还观察到一个有趣现象：许多AI领军人物都有物理学背景，部分原因是希格斯玻色子发现后，高能物理领域的人才大量转向机器学习。

Liam Fedus, a co-creator of ChatGPT and former VP of Post-Training at OpenAI, has founded Periodic Labs to build AI foundation models specifically for materials science and chemistry — essentially applying frontier AI to the physical world of atoms. Surprisingly, Fedus argues that the AI revolution cannot fully accelerate scientific progress until these systems are directly connected to physical experimentation, not just language. Drawing on his physics background and early work at Google Brain (where he contributed to mixture-of-experts architectures and the transformer era), he contends that only recent advances in reasoning, test-time inference, and agentic tool use have made this physical-world AI vision technically feasible. He also notes an interesting pattern: many AI leaders share physics backgrounds, partly due to a post-Higgs-boson talent migration from high-energy physics into machine learning.
